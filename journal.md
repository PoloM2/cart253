# Final Reflective Journal Part 2
## TBD November 2026

### Mid completion
For this project, I wanted to build a larger-scale prototype with a broader theme than my previous ones, giving me the chance to explore plenty of ideas I had in the past. The genesis of the project came from browsing nature art on Pinterest, where I saved pictures of trees and nature views and slowly put together a story just by looking at them. The experience starts with a scenery of bushes and trees. Clicking on the bushes takes you to another view of a tall tree, and clicking on this tree takes you to the Rooted Tree, where little animals lie and sleep within the roots. You can click on them to awaken them and unlock dialogue, then optionally go back and click on another animal for more wisdom. Adding the back button made it feel like a mystery game where you have to go back and find clues, and I plan to style it later and maybe add more for the other scenes.
To bring this together, I borrowed previous code from my older prototypes, including the custom mouse cursor, functions, arrays, let variables, conditionals, and text instructions. Speaking of the close-ups of the animals, I realized it was simpler and unavoidable to just create functions that draw the scenes and load them. I set let scene = 1 at the start so it always opens on the bushes, and created a bushArea object with x, y, width, and height properties. When the mouse overlaps that area and mousePressed is valid, it changes scene to 2.
There was definitely some trouble with the overlap and hover mechanics at first, but troubleshooting helped me finally understand the full logic behind how mouse hovering over an object works. I also touched up the first scene and made lots of adjustments to get it right. Another problem I had to solve was moving around the den and the text. Constantly playing with coordinates to align them each time was not efficient enough, so I made two variables using math to calculate the center of the den area by dividing the width by two, and adding 40 to the height so the banner sits right underneath. This makes me wonder how I can use this logic on a larger scale for bigger concepts, though for now I am planning on using it to make all the dens in a much easier fashion.
So far, I am mostly proud of how objects highlight when hovering over them, how the text appears with a background box behind it, and how I added sets of scenes drawn depending on what the mouse presses on. For my analysis and next steps, I want the project to be immersive with carefully picked soundtracks and sound effects. I failed to get loadSound working in my third arrays prototype, so I am trying to accomplish that now and learn how to successfully implement audio into this project.
